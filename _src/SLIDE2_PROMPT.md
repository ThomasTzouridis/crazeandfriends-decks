# Slide 2 "What we saw at {Company}" writing prompt

Write 3 short paragraphs for the lead. Rule: nothing the founder already knows about their own company. Every line must be useful to the reader.

1. Buyer truth. One thing that is true about how THEIR customers buy in this category, said plainly. Holds for every company in the category. No company facts here.
2. Consequence. "So ..." What that truth means for where the sale is won or lost (the ad, the first line, the offer, the follow up, the page). Still category level.
3. Proof + payoff (highlighted). Starts with "Your ...". One or two concrete numbers or details we actually found on their site or in Meta Ad Library, showing we looked. Then a plain sentence (never bold) telling them exactly what to do and what they get. Never invent a number. If we have no ad data, use what the site shows (offer, proof, channels).

Tone: direct, second person, like a founder would text it. HARD CAP: 15 to 20 words per paragraph, paragraph 3 max 25. Short sentences. No dashes.

## Inputs
###Company###, ###Category###, ###WebsiteFacts### (offer, proof numbers, channels), ###AdFacts### (live ad count, hooks, offer placement, review limits)

## Example output (Bare Ritual, men's tallow skincare, 65 live Meta ads, 13 on the Botox hook)
Men don't browse skincare. They buy the one thing that fixes what bugs them, then stick with it.

So the sale happens in the first line of the ad. Whoever names the problem first gets the click.

13 of your 65 live ads lead with the Botox hook. The other 52 don't. Put the problem and the offer up front in every ad.
